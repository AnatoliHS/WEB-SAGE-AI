(Concepts) The idea

Five product-style ways to show the three steps.

Each design replaces the 01–03 rows under "Software you can make, not just use." They all use the three real Tinker screenshots and the current copy. Scroll slowly, hover, and try a narrow window.

  • A Sticky Showcase
  • B Tabbed Window
  • C Prompt Timeline
  • D Blueprint
  • E 3D Deck
  • Compare
A

Sticky Showcase

The classic product-site scroll story, as on Linear, Vercel and Stripe. The steps scroll past on the left along a progress rail, while one app window stays pinned on the right and swaps to each step's screenshot as you reach it. On phones each screenshot sits under its step.

  • Scroll-driven
  • Pinned window
  • Progress rail

B

Tabbed Window

One wide panel with the three steps as tabs, in the style of Raycast or Arc. A thin bar under the active tab fills over 6 seconds, then the screenshot slides to the next step and the copy cross-fades. Click any tab to jump, and hover to pause. It's compact and uses the least vertical space.

  • Compact
  • Auto-advancing
  • Slide transition

C

Prompt Timeline

A vertical timeline whose line fills from blue to orange as you scroll. Each step lights up its node and plays a short dark "prompt log" showing what you'd actually type and what Tinker answers. The screenshots tilt in 3D toward the cursor.

  • Developer-tool feel
  • Shows real prompts
  • 3D tilt

D

Blueprint

An engineering-drawing panel with a grid background and dashed frames. When each screenshot scrolls into view, orange markers pop onto the parts of the interface that matter, such as "Describe it in your words" and "Live preview", like annotated specs. Rows alternate sides.

  • Annotated UI
  • Explains the product
  • Dark panel

E

3D Deck

The three screenshots stand in a perspective stack, like cards on a desk. Choosing a step, or waiting 5 seconds, flips the front card up and away and brings the next one forward. The step list beside it works as an accordion, so only the active step shows its description.

  • Most futuristic
  • Depth & perspective
  • Accordion steps

Recommendation: C, Prompt Timeline. It's the most tech-flavoured, and the prompt logs show how plain-language Tinker is without extra copy. It also echoes the blue-to-orange of the logo. B is the safe, compact choice if the page is getting long. D's annotations could also be layered onto C later.

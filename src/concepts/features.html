(Concepts) Features

Five ways to make the features come alive.

Each layout gives every feature its own colour, which slides into its frame on hover, in the spirit of Outfit. Hover over everything, and try a narrow window too. All nine features and their copy are unchanged.

  • A Colour Tiles
  • B Index Rows
  • C Bento
  • D Strips
  • E Spotlight
  • Compare
A

Colour Tiles

The current three-column grid becomes nine framed tiles. On hover, the colour slides in from whichever edge your cursor came from and leaves through the edge it exits. The icon tilts and an arrow appears. It's the closest match to Outfit's hover feel.

  • Direction-aware slide
  • Familiar grid
  • Lowest risk

Index Rows

An editorial list with big titles, matching the numbered rows in "The idea". On hover, the row's colour wipes in from the left, the title glides right and an arrow appears. On leaving, the colour exits to the right, like a page turning.

D

Strips

Nine tall strips stand side by side with their titles running vertically. The hovered strip widens smoothly, colour pours down from the top, and its description fades in. Hovering across the row feels like flicking through a rack of fabric swatches. On phones it becomes an accordion that opens with a tap.

  • Expanding panels

E

Spotlight

A list of feature names sits beside one large colour frame. Hovering a name rolls the frame, like a slot machine, to that feature's colour, big icon and description. The frame is always visible and never pops up at the cursor. Out of view it auto-plays, so phones get the effect too.

  • One big frame
  • Rolls vertically
  • Auto-plays on phones

Recommendation: A, Colour Tiles, or B, Index Rows for a more editorial feel. Both keep all nine features readable at a glance, and their hover is pure Outfit. A swaps straight into the current grid. B echoes the numbered rows in "The idea". If you want the section to be a showpiece, D is the most memorable.
